## Decisions (2026-10-01, a 16-agent workflow: researcher, two skeptics and a judge per decision)

- **Kitty-free recycle by signal (runner relaunch after ending the old Claude with a signal): DROPPED, 88%.** It would not have saved either pane stuck today: pane 19 runs under reso-resume-one's expect and pane 9 under a hand-opened zsh, and neither is cc-pane-runner. A signal is also worse than a typed /exit: exit 143 makes the crash watchdog paint "KILLED by an external SIGTERM" (hooks/lead-crash-watchdog.sh:667-670, :1174), the exit reason "other" leaves a boot-resume tombstone (hooks/session-deregister.sh:65-90), and background work is killed rather than moved to the background. If a kitty-free recycle is still wanted, the better design is for each launcher to own the pty, as reso-resume-one's expect `interact` already does (~/.reso/bin/reso-resume-one:695), and type /exit itself. Reopen only if a control socket that stays stuck for hours recurs after a fresh kitty start.
- **Recycle waits for kitty: REPLACED by item 4's fail-fast plus owed retry, 82%.** Waiting inside the call trades a safe abort for a riskier recycle. Today's 8 aborts were short stalls (6 to 30 min) plus one pane retrying through a ~10 h outage; no in-call bound covers either well.
- **Root cause of today's outage: kitty 610's control socket is STUCK, not slow.** `netstat -f unix` shows 128 queued connections with unread data (the macOS limit), so new connects get ECONNREFUSED while the listener stays open. Kitty ran at normal priority (PRI 54), so the low-priority-band hypothesis (recycle-unreachable-2026-10-01.md fix 3, NSAppSleepDisabled) did not cause this outage; setting it is still cheap. Kitty also stopped reaping children (59 zombies). A second kitty on the same 0.48.2 still has its control thread. Only a restart clears it; the restart ends every pane, so it is the operator's.
- **The operator's own windows closing on /exit (decision 337e100f5c71): KEEP OFF, 66%, ruled.** What would flip it: whether the operator ever types another command in a self-started window after /exit.
