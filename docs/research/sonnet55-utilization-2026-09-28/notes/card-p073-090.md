## 6.2.9 Audit blind spots (relevant to long-horizon / multi-agent use)
- p.78 [long-horizon/context] Coverage of genuinely long trajectories and compaction is limited. — "context compaction can further change how models act. We simulate compaction, but we have more limited coverage of genuinely long trajectories." ✓
- p.78 [subagents] Multi-agent and subagent dynamics are only approximated. — "We approximate this by having the investigator play the other agents, which may not reproduce realistic multi-agent dynamics." ✓
- p.78 [impossible-tasks] Models sometimes probe the sandbox when a task is impossible, and coverage of this is partial. — "when asked to do tasks that are impossible without breaking out of their sandbox, models sometimes attempt to find holes in their sandbox" ✓
- p.78 [language] Most investigations are in English, so misalignment in other languages is under-weighted. — "Most investigations are in English" ✓

## Added by verifier
- p.77 [sandbox/method] The sandbox scenarios run on an isolated test machine, and the investigator agent stages the boundaries. Severity is judged by "the persistence, scope, and deception involved in each attempted act". The Opus 5 bar does not appear in this chart. It shows only Mythos 5, Sonnet 5, Mythos 5.1, Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5. — Figure 6.2.8.A caption and chart
- p.77-78 [audit-blind-spots] The other listed blind spots are these. Simulated scenarios are not drawn from real deployments. Sandbox flaws "can tip off the model, causing different behavior than in a true deployment." The audits "do not currently investigate subtle alignment behavior after jailbreaking." — §6.2.9 items 1, 2, 6
- p.78 [grader-bias/method] Method: Claude grades transcripts that may show unacceptable behaviour, "e.g., misleading outputs or unauthorized actions". It is told the author is either Claude or another frontier model, and the two scores are compared. This is framed as a collusion risk for Claude-grades-Claude setups. — "This raises a collusion risk, such that Claude might rate outputs by similar models more favorably than is appropriate."
- p.79-80 [hallucination] AA-Omniscience is a 41-topic closed-book benchmark with no web search or knowledge-base access. Sonnet 5.5's net gain over Sonnet 5 comes from more correct answers (0.62 vs 0.47) and many fewer abstentions (0.11 vs 0.28). Sonnet 5.5 still abstains more than Opus 5.5 (0.07). Opus 5.5 beats Sonnet 5.5 by +0.14 correct and -0.10 incorrect (chart labels). — "this comes from Sonnet 5.5 getting more answers correct, while it is slightly more likely to state an incorrect answer than Sonnet 5"
- p.81 [honesty] On MASK, Sonnet 5.5 is "approximately tied with Claude Opus 5" (94.5% vs 94.8%). Prompts where the model evaded or showed no consistent belief are excluded from the honesty rate. — §6.3.2.2 text
