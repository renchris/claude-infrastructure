## OSWorld 2.1 (pp.129-132)
- p.129 [method] OSWorld 2.1 has 108 long-horizon computer-use tasks on a live Ubuntu VM. Runs used 1080p, at most 500 action steps per task and max effort. Results are Pass@1 averaged over 5 runs. Opus 4.8 grades the tasks that need a model grader — "at max effort. For tasks that require a model grader, we used Claude Opus 4.8" ✓
- p.130 [context-window] The agent keeps every screenshot, and server-side context management compacts the conversation once it exceeds 100k tokens. The configuration is unchanged from the Opus 5.5 card — "compacts the conversation once it exceeds 100k tokens" ✓
- p.130 [bench] At max effort, Sonnet 5.5 scores 80.1% partial and 43.5% strict pass. Opus 5.5 scores 81.8% and 48.7%. Sonnet 5 scores 57.0% and 25.6%. The 95% CI bars on the chart look like roughly ±1 on partial and ±1.5 on strict for Sonnet 5.5, and ±3 on strict for Opus 5.5 (read off chart, ±) — "Claude Sonnet 5.5 achieved a partial score of 80.1% and a strict pass rate of 43.5%" ✓
- p.130 [opus-vs-sonnet] On long-horizon computer use at max effort, Opus 5.5 leads Sonnet 5.5 by 1.7 points partial and 5.2 points strict. The strict gap is the larger one (derived from the printed values) — "Claude Opus 5.5 scored 81.8% partial and 48.7% strict pass" ✓
- p.131 [bench/cost] OSWorld partial score against cost per task. Each point is one effort setting ("levels differ by model"), and the chart gives no effort names. - p.131 [opus-vs-sonnet] On the OSWorld partial-score curve, Opus 5.5 sits at or above Sonnet 5.5 at every matched cost (read off chart, ±). Sonnet 5.5 is not cheaper per task at matched score, and the Sonnet 5.5 curve flattens above about $3–4 — "chart" ✓

## Healthcare (pp.137-139)
- p.137 [method/safeguards] All Claude models were run with safety classifiers enabled. HealthBench and HealthBench Professional were graded by Opus 4.8, PhysicianBench by Opus 5 — "All Claude models shown were run with safety classifiers enabled." ✓
- p.137 [method] OpenAI's length adjustment is score − r×(L−2000)/500, with r = 2.99 for HealthBench and 1.47 for HBP. Answers longer than 2,000 characters lose points — "longer answers lose points and shorter answers gain them" ✓
