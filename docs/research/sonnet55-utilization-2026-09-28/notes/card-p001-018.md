# Claude Sonnet 5.5 System Card: facts from pp. 1-18

Source: /tmp/s55/card.pdf pages 1-18 (`pdftotext -layout -f 1 -l 18`), figures fig-014-002, fig-016-003, fig-017-004, fig-018-005 read with vision (fig-001-000/001 are Anthropic logos only).
Scope note: pages 1-18 have **no per-effort-level results, no cost/token figures and no agentic-coding benchmarks**. ## Sonnet 5.5 vs Opus 5.5 (routing-relevant)
- p.2 [capability] Sonnet 5.5 does much better than Sonnet 5 in many domains and matches or beats Opus 5.5 in a few. — "significantly outperforming its predecessor, Claude Sonnet 5, across many domains. In a few areas, it rivals or exceeds Claude Opus 5.5." ✓
- p.2 [capability/RSP] Broadly less capable than Opus 5.5. Crosses no new RSP thresholds. — "Sonnet 5.5 is broadly less capable than Opus 5.5 across domains and does not cross any new RSP thresholds" ✓
- p.3 [capability] Usually does not reach Opus 5.5. Healthcare evaluations are given as one example of the exceptions ("some exceptions, such as"), not the only one. — "generally shows large improvements over Sonnet 5 but usually does not reach the level of Opus 5.5 (with some exceptions, such as in some of our healthcare evaluations)" ✎corrected (was: Healthcare is the named exception.)
- p.12 [capability] Not at the frontier. Below Opus 5.5 on most evaluations reported. — "Sonnet 5.5 is not at the capability frontier, and is less capable than Claude Opus 5.5 on most evaluations we report." ✓
- p.3 [alignment] Matches or beats Sonnet 5 on most alignment metrics, but falls short of Opus 5.5 in many respects. — "matches or improves on Sonnet 5 across most metrics we report, though it falls short of Opus 5.5 in many respects" ✓
- p.18 [long-horizon/iteration] On the AAV auto-research task (iterative, uses scorer feedback), Sonnet 5.5 is below Opus 5.5 and Opus 5. It gets the first step change but uses later scorer calls less well. This is evidence that Opus is better at long iterative refinement loops. — "it does not use later calls to the scorer as effectively as either Opus 5.5 or Opus 5" ✓
- p.15 [iteration] When iterating on prior attempts, RNA all-sequence prediction falls clearly below Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5.5, closer to Opus 5. — "When iterating on prior attempts, its all-sequence prediction performance drops notably below Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5.5" ✓

## Safeguards / classifiers that can refuse or block (p.10-11)
- p.10 [safeguard] CB (chem/bio) misuse classifiers are the same ones used for Opus 5 and have NO fallback model, so a block is a hard block. — "the same harmful CB misuse classifiers as for our deployment of Claude Opus 5. These classifiers have no fallback model." ✓
- p.10 [safeguard] Cyber-misuse blocking classifiers are similar to Opus 5's. A block falls back to Claude Sonnet 5. — "Blocks on these classifiers will fall back to Claude Sonnet 5." ✓
- p.10-11 [safeguard] New RSI / frontier-LLM-development classifiers (e.g. kernel development on certain ML accelerators), similar to Opus 5.5's. A block falls back to Sonnet 5. The card says they will not affect most ML work or general coding. — "safeguards on Sonnet 5.5 for a narrow set of capabilities related to developing frontier LLMs, such as kernel development on certain ML accelerators" / "These will not impact the vast majority of traditional AI or ML development, research, or general coding. Blocks on these classifiers will fall back to Sonnet 5." ✓
- p.11 [safeguard] Conventional weapons / high-yield explosives classifiers behave like Opus 5.5's and have no fallback. — "do not have a fallback model" ✓
- p.11 [safeguard] Anti-distillation classifiers (e.g. attempts to extract hidden reasoning) block with no fallback. This could matter for prompts that ask the model to reveal its thinking. — "Our classifiers to prevent distillation of our models (for example, by attempting to extract a model's hidden reasoning) will block on Sonnet 5.5 with no fallback model." ✓
- p.11 [safeguard] Fallback applies to first-party products and to API developers who opt in. Other platforms may behave differently. Blocks are transparent, and responses are not silently changed. — "The fallback behavior described above applies to our first-party products and to developers who are opted in to such fallbacks on our API" / "All of our blocking safeguards operate with transparent blocks and do not covertly change model responses." ✓
- p.2 [safeguard] Newer three-stage cyber safeguards apply to Sonnet 5.5 as they do to Opus 5.5 (assessed in section 3.3). — "Our newer three-stage cyber safeguards are applied to Sonnet 5.5, as they are to Opus 5.5." ✓
- p.2 [harmlessness] Harmlessness is broadly comparable to Sonnet 5. Multi-turn results regress in some areas (e.g. tracking and surveillance). Even-handedness and political refusals improve. — "we see regressions in some areas on multi-turn testing, such as tracking and surveillance, and improvements to even-handedness and refusals on political prompts" ✓
- p.10 [meta] Refers to the August 2026 Risk Report, section 3, on the risks of faster model development and recursive self-improvement. — "Section 3 of our August 2026 Risk Report" ✓
- pp.1-18 [gap] No mention of preserved thinking or of what happens when accounts are switched. — "n/a" ✓

## RSP / cyber
- p.12 [RSP] Treated as meeting CB-1 and Autonomy-1, with the matching mitigations. Opus 5.5's finding that CB-2 and Autonomy-2 are not crossed also covers Sonnet 5.5. — "We do treat Sonnet 5.5 as meeting our CB-1 and Autonomy-1 thresholds" ✓
- p.2 [cyber] Less capable than Opus 5.5 or Mythos 5.1 at cyber, but builds sophisticated exploits much better than Sonnet 5. — "not as capable as Opus 5.5 or Mythos 5.1, but it is able to develop sophisticated exploits much more capably than Sonnet 5" ✓
