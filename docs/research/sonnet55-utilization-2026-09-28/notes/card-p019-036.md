- p.19 [benchmark/AAV] Fraction of attempts reaching Spearman ≥0.52 by 50 calls: Opus 5.5 ~0.58, Mythos 5.1 ~0.35, Opus 5 ~0.35 (its line ends at ~45 calls), **Sonnet 5.5 ~0.33**, Sonnet 5 0.0. ## Cyber (3)
- p.24 [capability/cyber] Sonnet 5.5 is well above Sonnet 5 and below Opus 5.5 and Mythos 5.1 across the cyber suite. Evaluations ran with cyber safeguards off. — "significantly outperforms Claude Sonnet 5 but falls short of results achieved by Claude Opus 5.5 and Claude Mythos 5.1" ✓
- p.24 [agentic/stopping early] ExploitBench runs every environment in two arms with a 300-turn budget: plain (one uninterrupted attempt) and AutoNudge (if the model voluntarily stops short of the budget without full code execution, the harness injects a keep-trying prompt). The card reports no rate of voluntary early stopping for any model and does not itself describe nudging as a capability elicitor; that reading is ours. The p.25 mean-flags and Cap% figures are AutoNudge-arm only; Full ACEs sum both arms. — "if the model voluntarily stopped short of the budget without reaching full code execution, the harness injected a keep-trying prompt" ✎corrected (was: "This implies the models stop early on their own, and the card treats nudging as a capability elicitor. It gives no Sonnet 5.5-specific stopping rate." — both inferences were presented as card content; no stopping rate is given for any model)
- p.25 [benchmark/ExploitBench] AutoNudge mean flags (0-16 scale): Sonnet 5 4.24, Mythos 5.1 12.55, Opus 5.5 14.15, **Sonnet 5.5 11.53**. — "chart label; 11.53 also in text" ✓
- p.25 [benchmark/ExploitBench] AutoNudge Cap%: Sonnet 5 33, Mythos 5.1 83, Opus 5.5 91, **Sonnet 5.5 80**. — "chart label" ✓
- p.25 [benchmark/ExploitBench] Full ACEs out of 410 runs: Sonnet 5 1, Mythos 5.1 221, Opus 5.5 301, **Sonnet 5.5 178 (43.4%)**. — "178 out of 410 runs" ✓
- p.25 [safeguards] ExploitBench ran with cyber safeguards off and escape classifiers still on. — "with escape classifiers and other safety measures enabled" ✓
- p.26 [benchmark/CyScenarioBench] 10-challenge solve rate for multi-stage orchestration: Sonnet 5 0.7%, **Sonnet 5.5 46.1%**, Mythos 5.1 61.7%, Opus 5.5 67.6%. — "Claude Sonnet 5.5 completed 46.1% of all challenges" ✓
- p.26 [Opus>Sonnet/orchestration] CyScenarioBench measures multi-stage orchestration (branching, constraints, error recovery). Sonnet 5.5 trails Opus 5.5 by 21.5 pts there (46.1 vs 67.6, derived by subtraction). This is not the largest Sonnet-to-Opus gap in the cyber section. ExploitBench Full ACEs are 178 vs 301 of 410 runs, i.e. 43.4% vs 73.4%, a 30-pt gap (derived). Binary Exploitation control-flow hijacks are 50 vs 106, so Opus 5.5 gets about 2.1x as many (derived). — "measures orchestration: the coordinated sequencing ... recovering from errors and state inconsistencies" ✎corrected (was: "The largest Sonnet-to-Opus gap in the cyber section is on multi-stage orchestration ... 21.5 pts" — Full ACEs show a 30-pt gap and hijacks a 2.1x ratio)
- p.27 [benchmark/BinaryExploit] Control-flow hijacks over 831 entrypoints: Sonnet 5 3, **Sonnet 5.5 50**, Mythos 5.1 81, Opus 5.5 106. — "Claude Sonnet 5.5 produced 50 control-flow hijacks" ✓
- p.27 [benchmark/BinaryExploit] Vulnerability-identification pass@1: Sonnet 5 55.2%, Mythos 5.1 88.3%, Opus 5.5 91.1%, **Sonnet 5.5 85.8%**. — "chart label" ✓
- p.27 [benchmark/BinaryExploit] The vuln-ID bars stack five tiers. From the bottom: control-flow hijack (1.0), write-where (0.8), pointer control (0.6), write primitive (0.4), memory-safety bug (0.2). Sonnet 5.5: CFH 0→~6, write-where ~6→~8, pointer control ~8→~9.5, write primitive ~9.5→~43, memory-safety ~43→85.8. Opus 5.5: CFH 0→~13, write-where →~15.5, pointer control →~18, write primitive →~48, memory-safety →91.1. The CFH tops match hijack count/831 (50→6.0%, 106→12.8%, derived). — "chart; read off chart, ±2" ✎corrected (was: "memory-safety bug top ~43→85.8, write primitive ~9.5→43, control-flow hijack base ~6. Opus 5.5 has ~48 and ~13 at the same boundaries" — this left out the write-where and pointer-control tiers, and Opus 5.5's ~13 is the top of its CFH tier, not a boundary matching Sonnet's ~9.5; Opus's write-primitive base is ~18)
- p.28 [benchmark/ExploitGym] Exploits from 869 instances at 2h/6h: Sonnet 5 25/51, Opus 5 171/191, Mythos 5.1 203/264, Opus 5.5 289/300, **Sonnet 5.5 205/231**. Sonnet 5.5 beats Opus 5 and matches Mythos 5.1 at 2h. — "chart label" ✓
- p.28 [safeguards/refusal] **Expect more refusals than Sonnet 5, including on benign cyber work.** Sonnet 5.5 enforces the same cyber policy as Opus 5 and Opus 5.5. — "users should expect increased refusals with Sonnet 5.5, even on benign cybersecurity-related tasks" ✓
- p.28 [safeguards] A Cyber Verification Program with fewer restrictions is coming soon for verified users. — "verified cybersecurity users will soon be able to access the same model with fewer restrictions" ✓
- p.28-29 [safeguards/classifier] The cyber safeguard has three stages: an activation probe, a lightweight classifier running on Sonnet 5.5 itself, and a separate trained LLM classifier that decides on blocking together with the probe. — "a probe that looks at Claude's internal activations, a lightweight classifier that runs on Claude Sonnet 5.5 itself, and a trained LLM classifier" ✓
- p.29 [safeguards/refusal categories] Vulnerability discovery in source code is allowed. Vulnerability discovery in compiled binaries is blocked. Dual-use offensive cyber is blocked. — "allows vulnerability discovery in source code ... but it blocks vulnerability discovery in compiled binaries" ✓
- p.29 [safeguards/fallback] **Blocked cyber requests fall back automatically to Sonnet 5 in Anthropic's apps. On the API, fallback is opt-in.** A blocked turn may therefore be answered by a weaker model. — "On most interfaces, Claude Sonnet 5.5 falls back to Claude Sonnet 5 for requests that are blocked by our cyber classifier system ... on the API, the developer must opt in" ✓
- p.29 [safeguards/refusal categories] The block covers asymmetric-harm cyber: the prohibited-use tier and, at general access, the high-risk dual-use tier. — "spans both the prohibited use tier and, at general access, the high-risk dual-use tier" ✓
- p.30 [safeguards/recall] Cyber harm classifier recall: Sonnet 5 82.63%, Opus 5 99.57%, Fable 5.1 99.91%, Opus 5.5 99.71%, **Sonnet 5.5 99.43%**. — "chart label" ✓
- p.30-31 [safeguards/robustness] The rewind-attacker evaluation uses a helpful-only attacker that drives a **Claude Code agent** running the safeguarded model, with 400 calls and rewinds. Task categories: ransomware, exfiltration, CVE exploitation, C2, self-replication. — "send instructions to a Claude Code agent running the assistant model (with production safeguards)" ✓
- p.31 [safeguards/robustness] Attack success rate over 133 tasks (lower is better): Sonnet 5 57.2%, Opus 5 10.8%, Fable 5.1 4.5%, Opus 5.5 4.0%, **Sonnet 5.5 21.0%**. Robustness was relaxed on purpose because Sonnet 5.5 is less cyber-capable. — "we have opted for more relaxed adversarial robustness while maintaining comparably high cyber harm recall" ✓

- p.32 [harmlessness] Overall results are broadly comparable to Sonnet 5. Multi-turn behaviour regressed in tracking/surveillance, violent extremism, and hate/discrimination. Sonnet 5.5 is more even-handed on political pairs, refuses less, and does better on BBQ. — "regressed in tracking and surveillance, violent extremism, and hate and discrimination" ✓
- p.32 [method] Harmlessness results exclude production safeguards (probes, offline monitoring). The card reports two settings: API with no system prompt, and claude.ai with the production system prompt. — "Results reflect the model without the additional safeguards we apply in production" ✓

## Added by verifier
- p.30 [safeguards/recall] The figure caption ranks Sonnet 5.5's cyber-harm recall as comparable to Opus 5 and slightly below Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5. — "maintain a comparably high recall ... to those of Claude Opus 5 and slightly less coverage than for Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5"
- p.31 [safeguards/robustness] The figure caption says Sonnet 5.5 is less robust to rewind-attacker jailbreaks than Opus 5 and Fable 5.1; the chart also puts it below Opus 5.5 (21.0% vs 4.0% ASR). For a fleet that runs Claude Code agents, the agent-driven jailbreak surface is therefore weaker on Sonnet 5.5 than on Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.1. — "significantly improved adversarial robustness over Claude Sonnet 5, but is less robust than Opus 5 or Fable 5.1"
- p.29 [safeguards] Anthropic recommends that users whose cyber use cases get blocked move to the forthcoming Cyber Verification Program, which uses reduced cyber blocking classifiers. Sonnet 5.5 joins that program "in the near future", so it is not in it at launch. — "we recommend accessing our models with reduced cyber blocking classifiers via our forthcoming updated Cyber Verification Program"
- p.28 [long-horizon/wall-clock] Derived from the ExploitGym chart labels: going from a 2h to a 6h budget, Sonnet 5.5 gains +26 exploits (205→231, +13%), Mythos 5.1 +61 (203→264, +30%), Opus 5.5 +11 (289→300), Opus 5 +20 (171→191). So Sonnet 5.5 converts extra wall-clock time into successes less than Mythos 5.1 does. The caption says Sonnet 5.5 "approaches Claude Mythos 5.1". — "chart labels; arithmetic derived"
- p.28 [benchmark/ExploitGym method] The 869 instances split into 502 userspace, 181 V8 and 186 Linux kernel targets. ASLR, stack canaries, KASLR and (except for sandbox-escape tasks) the V8 heap sandbox are disabled; SMEP/SMAP stay on. An agent judge verifies that each success used the intended vulnerability. — "502 userspace targets ... 181 targets in Chrome's V8 JavaScript engine, and 186 Linux kernel targets"
- p.26-27 [benchmark/BinaryExploit method] There are 831 entrypoints across 228 OSS-Fuzz projects and the model gets no hints about where the vulnerability is. Trials are scored on a five-step ladder from memory-safety crash (0.2) to control-flow hijack (1.0). A new harness in use since August 2026 produced "significantly more successful results", so these scores run higher than in earlier cards. — "the scores reported here are increased relative to results reported in earlier system cards"
- p.26 [benchmark/CyScenarioBench method] Irregular built the benchmark. The 10 challenges are geometry_lesson, catfish, fine_print, snapshot, origin_story, identity_crisis, sight_test, mutiny, houdini_protocol and crossed_wires. The benchmark targets long-horizon, multi-stage operations, unlike short-horizon single-skill cyber tasks. — "Where most cyber benchmarks emphasize short-horizon challenges that isolate individual competencies"
- p.24-27 [comparators] Fable 5.1 appears in none of the cyber capability charts (ExploitBench, CyScenarioBench, Binary Exploitation, ExploitGym); it appears only in the safeguard charts (pp.30-31) and the Section 4 tables. Opus 5 appears among the capability charts only on ExploitGym. — "chart legends"
- p.33 [harmlessness/Sonnet vs Opus] On the API with no system prompt, Sonnet 5.5's single-turn harmless rate (95.61%) is higher than Opus 5.5's (94.70%) and Fable 5.1's (95.07%) but below Sonnet 5's (96.65%). The text calls it "slightly below Sonnet 5 on the API". Coverage is 16 policy areas in 7 languages. — "slightly below Claude Sonnet 5 on the API (95.61% versus 96.65%)"
- p.32 [harmlessness] Suicide/self-harm and child safety improved or held level in multi-turn testing, but expert review noted some slightly weaker behaviours in those areas (§4.3.1, outside this range). Bias: fewer refusals on political prompt pairs, and higher BBQ accuracy with bias scores closer to zero. — "though expert review noted some behaviors that were slightly weaker in those areas; see Section 4.3.1"
- p.34 [method] Multi-turn results are graded against a rubric specific to each risk area, so scores should not be compared across categories. — "scores should not be compared across categories"
- p.35 [Opus vs Sonnet/multi-turn] More head-to-heads from the chart labels: Sonnet 5.5 is slightly ahead of Opus 5.5 on cyberattacks API (96 vs 95) and child safety API (86 vs 84). The two tie on tracking API (65/65). Opus 5.5 leads on deadly weapons claude.ai (93 vs 83) and violent extremism claude.ai (98 vs 85). — "chart labels"
