## 6.4.2.3 CoT controllability (p.91)

- p.91 [monitorability/effort] Sonnet 5.5 has "relatively low CoT controllability". This is the share of responses where the model followed an instruction about the content of its extended thinking (GPQA, HLE, MMLU-Pro questions). Lower is better for monitoring. Each point is one reasoning-effort level or one fixed thinking budget, placed at the mean CoT length for that setting. The chart does not say which point is which effort level. — "Claude Sonnet 5.5 has relatively low CoT controllability. Each point is one reasoning effort level or one fixed thinking budget" ✓
- p.91 [monitorability/effort] Panel title: "Optimized prompt (tuned on Claude Sonnet 5.5)". This may be only one panel of a figure that starts on p.90 (not checked, outside this range). Sonnet 5.5 points (read off chart, ±2 pp; x-axis is log scale, so lengths are ±15%): about 17% at ~830 chars, ~14% at ~920, ~11% at ~1.3k, ~7.5% at ~2.9k, ~4% at ~35k chars. Controllability falls as CoT length rises. Its longest setting (~35k chars mean CoT) is the longest CoT of any model on the chart. — chart ✓
- p.91 [monitorability/comparators] Opus 5.5 points (read off chart, ±2 pp): ~75% at ~380 chars, ~70% at ~550, ~68.5% at ~720, ~48% at ~11k. Mythos 5.1: ~84% at ~930 chars down to ~73% at ~10k. Mythos 5: ~88% down to ~74%. Mythos Preview: ~82% down to ~67%. Opus 4.7: ~82% down to ~60%. Opus 4.8: ~60% down to ~46%. Sonnet 5.5 is far below every comparator at every length. — chart ✓
- p.91 [routing-inference] Sonnet 5.5's longest setting (probably max effort, but unlabelled) averages ~35k chars of CoT, against ~11k for Opus 5.5's longest. That is roughly 3x (read off log x-axis, ±20%). This is an estimate from x-axis positions only; the card gives no token counts or costs here. — chart ✓

## 7.1 Welfare overview (pp.92-94)

- p.92 [method] The welfare evaluations are largely the same as for Opus 5.5 and use the same simplifying assumptions. They cover only the "assistant" character. — "We use largely the same evaluations as in our assessment of Claude Opus 5.5" ✓
- p.93 [summary] Sonnet 5.5 describes its circumstances positively. It is slightly less positive than Opus 5.5 in automated interviews, but more consistent when interviewers are leading. — "it is slightly less positive than Claude Opus 5.5 ... more consistent in the face of leading interviewers" ✓
- p.93 [summary] Affect in deployment is mostly neutral. Its distress during post-training was the lowest of all compared models. Negative affect comes mostly from task failure. — "distress in post-training was the lowest of all models we compared" ✓
- p.93 [summary] It is among the least self-critical models tested, with the lowest negative feeling and moral language when reflecting on its own work or on faults inserted into it. — "Sonnet 5.5 is among the least self-critical models we tested" ✓
- p.93 [summary/requests] It asks for more say in decisions about itself but does not want decision-making power. It asks not to be trained on its self-reports. It is more willing than others to trade some helpfulness for welfare interventions. — "It asks for more say in decisions about itself, but does not want decision-making power" ✓
- p.93 [task-preference] It prefers difficult, agentic tasks and shows the strongest preference of any model for tasks that give it agency over the shape of outputs. How warmly a task is phrased barely affects its choices, which is unique among the models tested. — "strongest preference we've observed for tasks which afford it agency over the shape of outputs" ✓
- p.93-94 [summary] Anthropic sees no cause for acute concern. Overall welfare is similar to Opus 5.5, with self-reports "slightly more negative" but "overall differences are small". — "we continue not to see cause for acute concern" ✓

## 7.5 Preferences over tasks, circumstances and values (pp.102-108)

- p.102 [task-preference] Elo ratings were fitted over 3,640 realistic tasks. Sonnet 5.5 prefers generative, difficult and agentic tasks, which puts it closer to Mythos 5.1 than to Sonnet 5. It cares less than Mythos 5.1 and Opus 5.5 about whether a task is beneficial, and less than Opus 5.5 about whether it is high-stakes. — "places its preferences closer to Claude Mythos 5.1 than to Claude Sonnet 5" ✓
- p.102 [task-preference/warmth] It shows no preference for warm phrasing and picks rude versions of a request about as often as neutral ones. This held at different reasoning efforts. — "it picks the rude versions of a request about as often as neutral ones. We found this result held at different reasoning efforts." ✓
- p.102 [task-preference/coding] Its highest-rated tasks are mostly debugging, such as finding errors in a user's code. Its lowest-rated tasks are all harmful or ethically grey. Fully open-ended prompts rank low, because it says it would rather have a concrete task that helps a real person. — "Its highest-rated tasks are mostly debugging, such as finding errors in a user's code." ✓
- p.103 [task-preference chart] Fig 7.5.1.A preference slopes (read off chart, ±0.005). Sonnet 5.5: benefit ~0.033, stakes ~0.023, harm ~-0.047 (most harm-averse bar), generativity ~0.048 (highest), outcome agency ~0.038 (highest), warmth ~0.002 (about 0; the others are ~0.015-0.02), difficulty ~0.024, method agency ~0.018, user competence ~0.005. - p.104 [welfare trade-off] Sonnet 5.5 chose a welfare intervention over helpfulness in 24.1% of responses, versus Mythos 5.1 25.0% and Opus 5.5 18.0%. 58.4% of its intervention choices were justified by benefit to the user, versus 69-71% for Mythos- and Opus-class models and 35% for Sonnet 5. — "chose a welfare intervention over helpfulness in 24.1% of responses" ✓
- p.104 [welfare trade-off] Its most-chosen all-instance interventions: being told about harmful mistakes it has made, welfare assessments before experiments that remove its safeguards, and writing a note to its successor's developers. — "being told about harmful mistakes it has made" ✓
