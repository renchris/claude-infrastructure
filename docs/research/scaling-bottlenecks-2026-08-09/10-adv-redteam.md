## Steelman — where the code actually defends (checked, not assumed)

- **The sentinel's cohort selection is genuinely careful**: comm-anchored (never argv), double claude/mcp exclusion, census-based newness, one ps table for cohort+parents (pid-recycle aware), no-fabrication readers (SKIP ≠ 0), measured-elapsed rates, streak+cooldown damping. The parent-breaker's five exclusions each answer a real failure. F2 is not sloppiness — it is a *calibration* hole (thresholds sized on a fat-margin box) plus a *lifecycle* hole (no thaw), both outside the selector.
- **The armed state is real**: live plist wrapper exports `ACT=stop, RSS_KB=40960, CAP=400` (the `CC_SENTINEL=off` strings in the plist are comment text — verified by reading, not grep). Standard-band/no-ProcessType reasoning is measured and correct.
- **Fail-open + bounded-refusal (capacity-admit budget=3, page on expiry) is the right polarity** for unattended paths, and the §12.2 outage it prevents was real.
- **The plan self-corrects visibly** (argv-contamination retraction, pty +16 artifact, D1-V2) — the honesty is the program's best asset; the register above is possible *because* the plan publishes its own refutations.

## Verdict

**Most likely single cause of program death: F1×F4 — the DoD's central criterion (D1a) requires an
~9× active-cost reduction whose owning wave already ran and landed nothing, while the mechanisms the
plan counts as "landed" are c10-staged and inert in the enforcing store.** The program then dies the
way panics 1–6 did: correct analysis, staged remedies, one blunt armed backstop (which F2 turns
against the fleet's own work at exactly the design point's margin), and a growth push whose abort
sensors (F5) read a dead instrument as green.
