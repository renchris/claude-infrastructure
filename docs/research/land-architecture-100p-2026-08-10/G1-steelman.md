# G1 Steelman: the synchronous lock IS the 100th-percentile architecture

**Verdict: the queue proposal dissolves on a dilemma it cannot escape.** Either the enqueue-and-close daemon lands WITHOUT the author session — and inherits every judgment point the pipeline deliberately routes to the author — or it holds the entry until the author confirms, which IS the current design plus a daemon.

**1. Lands fail in author-shaped ways, routinely — not rarely.** ship-land's exit vocabulary is judgment, not plumbing: 3=escalation PARK, 5=rebase conflict, 6=red gate ("a claim about YOUR CODE, actionable"), 8=verify-retries exhausted → REVIEW. This morning's log tail alone shows two exit-6 red lands (`red:"bats-shellcheck"`, `red:"shellcheck,dead-assertion"`) inside 20 minutes — each fixed and re-landed by its author within minutes. A 3am daemon holding an exit-5/6 has three options: auto-resolve (semantic mis-merge, the exact 2026-07-11 dropped-commit class), strand it (worse than 📦 — the session already reported "enqueued" and closed on a promise), or page the operator (converts agent-fixable into human interrupt).

**2. Congestion is a rare tail on a ~5-10% utilized resource; a queue shortens nothing.** Observed tail: wait_s is 0-2 in the overwhelming majority (worst: 70s, 33s); real holds ~70-254s. Even 80s×124/day ≈ 2.8h/24h. A queue never reduces service time — it only relocates the wait, and the waiter (`cc-await` armed, event-driven) already makes waiting cost ~nothing; the 1h8m event was correctly self-diagnosed as tail contention. In exchange you buy a daemon SPOF — laptop sleep, QoS one-way ratchet, quota, crash, plus the bootstrap circle (the daemon lands the repo defining the daemon) — in a fleet whose memory index is a museum of daemon/liveness failure modes.

**3. Async landing breaks the un-fakeable close.** ✅ requires content-verify BY the author (`git ls-tree`/diff on YOUR paths — the 2026-07-11 lesson: count read 0 while files were absent). Only the session knows its DoD paths; a daemon can verify "a land happened," not "my work survived the rebase." Every close degrades to a promise about a future actor — the advisory-diode defect ("conclusion must reach the enforcing store") reinstalled at the fleet's most critical chokepoint. The lock, by contrast, holds zero state beyond itself and already self-reaps dead holders (pid+lstart).

**Falsifying checks (run these; queue dies if they read as predicted):**
1. **Wait-tail attribution** — parse land.log 7d: if p95 wait_s ≈ 0 and the 1h8m traces to one anomalous holder/coincidence, fix the anomaly (hold watchdog), not the architecture.
2. **Author-judgment rate** — fraction of land attempts exiting 3/5/6/8. If ≳10% (tail suggests yes), the queue strands or mis-resolves that fraction weekly.
3. **Close-integrity replay** — replay any exit-0 land as async: show what the ledger's ✅ rung reads at close-time. If it can only read "enqueued" (📦-forever) with no author-path content-verify, the certificate is structurally unreachable.
