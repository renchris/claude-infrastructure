## SKEPTIC OBJECTIONS
- THE WATCHER CAN BE KILLED BEFORE ITS 900 S PUT-BACK WAIT ENDS (measured). cc-reaper TERMs any launchd-parented bash older than 600 s unless its argv matches GARBAGE_WL (bin/cc-reaper:722), and that whitelist does not name handoff-fire or __recycle (bin/cc-reaper:945 is the orphan-bash rule). The recycle watcher is that kind of process: detach() starts it with start_new_session, then the python parent exits (handoff-fire.sh:1906-1913). Measured: `grep orphan-bash ~/.claude/logs/cc-reaper.log | grep __recycle` => `[2026-10-10T06:06:34Z] garbage: TERM orphan-bash pid=67561 age=613s argv=<bash …/handoff-fire.sh __recycle 11 …>`. So CC_RECYCLE_CARRY_WAIT_S=900, which hf_rcy_carry_putback counts from engagement, cannot be reached. A TERM during the cc_carry_putback loop runs only the :9664 trap, and that trap releases locks and nothing else. The recycle-composer-not-returned row and the RECYCLE-CARRY cc-notify are emitted only after cc_carry_putback returns, so both are lost. Any successor that works longer than about (600 s minus the watcher's age) loses its notification. The saved copy survives, named only in the foreground's recycle-composer-carried row. Residual risk 3 in the spec ('waits up to 900 s') is therefore wrong.
