## Patterns (for the methodology)

1. **The close answers a different question from the one the operator asks.** The fact-bound close (clean tree, landed, green) certifies the session's diff. The operator's "100.00/100.00" asks about the goal. In cases 0, 8, 9 and 10, "Good to close: yes" was literally true of the diff and false of the goal. A research-completeness claim needs its own verdict, computed against the frozen goal predicates, not against git.
2. **Research ends in a non-adversarial pass, and the operator's question supplies the adversary.** Every "no" began with the model switching from recall to an audit ("not from optimism", "a real audit, not an assertion"). The first adversarial pass always finds something because none ran before the claim. The fix is to make the refutation pass part of the claim itself (a red-team wave whose job is to find the next "one more thing"), so it runs before the operator asks.
3. **About a third of the gaps were already in the record.** Filed follow-ons, named loose ends, known losses and the plan's own prose all held them. Completeness was judged against a curated inventory (a tally, a row table, the ledger), while items sat outside it in prose, the backlog, peers' messages and logs. A completeness claim should enumerate every store (plan prose, backlog rows for the project, decision packets, logs, peer mail) and reconcile each item to done, or to a named not-required status with a reason.
4. **Instruments certify themselves.** The 0 🔧 tally, preflight PASS 46, the acceptance criteria and the findings-based parity check were each green over the defect. Each "complete" rested on an instrument never shown to go red on the gap it claims to rule out. Methodology: every completeness predicate needs a discriminating control (proven red on a planted gap) before it may carry a "done".
5. **Unchecked premises and blockers turn into operator questions.** Examples: The Key's tiers (the script existed), VIP 2 "mirror or correct" (a live defect), "confirmation email → SevenRooms" (no such step), relogin failing (the secret did not exist). Each premise was carried from prose into an ask without a read of the code or store that would settle it. A research phase should include a premise-verification pass over every open question before it is posed.
6. **Superlatives never turned into numbers.** "Maximally fast" had no ceiling measurement, so each re-ask found a lower ceiling (1.58× → Rust engine at 0.1% → JS-plugin composition unmeasured). The plan in case 0 had already concluded that "100th percentile" makes completion unfalsifiable. A finite research end needs the goal written as measurable predicates with ceilings, and a stop rule tied to them.
7. **Only a minority truly needed building or live probing.** Relogin never succeeding, the Chrome crash loop, six sidecar defects hidden behind fake-driven tests, deploys never executed, the 18-day dispatch hold. No amount of desk research finds these. A methodology that promises "no take-backs" upfront has to name this class and budget an execution probe (spike, live dry run, first armed run) inside the research phase, rather than letting it arrive as a surprise after "complete".
8. **Plans rot between sessions.** Findings were appended instead of written back into rows (case 3), open items were buried in 3,761 lines of prose (case 4), rows went stale when the operator deployed (case 2), and a predecessor's brief told the successor "do not go looking for more" (case 3). Research lost or narrowed across a recycle comes back later as a "new" gap.
