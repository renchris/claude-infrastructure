# C8 — driveItem change-notification latency and scope

**Verdict: partially_confirmed.** Four of five sub-claims hold verbatim. Sub-claim 4 (root-only) is **refuted for OneDrive personal** and confirmed only for OneDrive for Business. Sub-claim 5 understates the payload gap.

Primary sources (Microsoft Learn, fetched 2026-09-21; page `updated_at` 2026-04-07 / 2026-09-17 / 2026-05-14):
- A = /graph/change-notifications-overview
- B = /graph/api/resources/subscription?view=graph-rest-1.0
- C = /graph/api/subscription-post-subscriptions?view=graph-rest-1.0 (the `/onedrive/developer/rest-api/api/subscription_post_subscriptions` URL redirects here)
- D = /onedrive/developer/rest-api/concepts/using-webhooks (legacy OneDrive API doc, `ms.date` 2017-09-10)
- E = /onedrive/developer/rest-api/concepts/scan-guidance

## 1. Latency: max 6 hours, average under a minute — CONFIRMED
A and B, § Latency, identical tables: `| driveItem | Less than 1 minute | 6 hours |`. Column headers: "Average latency", "Maximum latency". The SharePoint `list` row carries the same pair.

## 2. Max expiration 42,300 minutes — CONFIRMED
A and B, § Subscription lifetime: `| OneDrive driveItem | 42,300 minutes (under 30 days) |`. Also B: "Any value under 45 minutes after the time of the request is automatically set to 45 minutes after the request time."
Conflict worth naming: D says "By default, subscriptions are set to expire 6 months from when they are created." That is the stale 2017 legacy-API page; the Graph pages are authoritative and carry the note "In the future, any requests to create or renew a subscription beyond the maximum value will fail."

## 3. Only `updated` — CONFIRMED (for the root item)
B, `changeType` property: "The supported values are: `created`, `updated`, `deleted`. … **Note:**- Drive root item and list change notifications support only the `updated` changeType."
E: "Drives support the “update” change type for webhooks. An “update” indicates that content within the drive has changed or that new content has been added or deleted."

## 4. Root only, no subfolders — REFUTED for personal, CONFIRMED for Business
C, § driveItem, decisive sentence: "On a personal OneDrive, you can subscribe to the root folder or any subfolder in that drive. On OneDrive for Business, you can subscribe to only the root folder. Change notifications are sent for the requested types of changes on the subscribed folder, or any file, folder, or other **driveItem** instances in its hierarchy. You can't subscribe to **drive** or **driveItem** instances that aren't folders, such as individual files."
A's Supported-resources table encodes the same asymmetry in prose while printing only root paths: personal = "Changes to content within the hierarchy of *any folder*: `/users/{id}/drive/root`"; business = "Changes to content within the hierarchy of the *root folder*: `/drives/{id}/root`, `/users/{id}/drive/root`".
Third-party scope error in the claim: a **SharePoint document library is not subscribed as driveItem at all** — it is the `list` resource, `/sites/{site-id}/lists/{list-id}` (A, C § Resources examples).

## 5. No payload; client must call delta — CONFIRMED, and stronger than stated
The notification carries no id of the changed item either. D: "You'll notice that the notification doesn't include any information about the changes that triggered it. Your app is expected to use the **delta** verb to detect any changes to the state of items in OneDrive and store the `syncToken` value for the next time you are notified." Its payload example is `subscriptionId`, `expirationDateTime`, `resource` (= `/me/drive/root`, the *subscribed* folder), `clientState`.
A classifies this shape: "**Basic notifications**: Change notifications that don't contain resource data other than the **id** of the resource that changed."
D also: "Multiple notifications to your service may be batched together into a single request". E prescribes the follow-up delta and "We recommend no more than once per day for this periodic check."
