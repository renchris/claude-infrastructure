## 1. What changed, and why

### The shape of the thing

`tests/lr-drill.sh` has two halves and they are separated by one gate, not by convention:

| half | verbs | who may run it |
|---|---|---|
| **PURE** | `--rows` · `--check-manifest` · `--verdict ARM DIR` · `--assert RESULTS` · `--stamp` | anyone; touches no pane, no account, no process |
| **LIVE** | `--seed` · `--drill MANIFEST\|last` | the operator only; spends quota, types into real panes, SIGKILLs a real process |

Everything that reaches a real pane, a real process, a real account or the service manager is
spelled inside **one function, `live_do`**, whose FIRST statement is `[ "$DRILL_ARMED" = 1 ] || …
return 90`. Only `--seed` and `--drill` set `DRILL_ARMED`. Two ratchets in the suite pin both
halves of that, and a third case pins that the guard actually REFUSES rather than merely being
present:

* **no real-pane verb outside `live_do`** — `it2` · `osascript` · `launchctl` · `kitty @` ·
  `kill -9`, matched over the WHOLE file including comments;
* **the armed check is `live_do`'s first executable statement** — not merely present;
* and behaviourally, every one of the seven recipes returns rc 90 while unarmed, writing nothing.

The whole-file scope of the first ratchet is a deliberate correction to my own first draft, and it
is the cheapest lesson in this wave. The draft filtered comments out before matching; a trailing
`# arm (b) — kill -9 the watcher …` on a function's own header line walked straight through it and
the ratchet reported clean. A comment stripper is a parser, and a parser inside a safety ratchet is
one more thing that can be wrong in the permissive direction. The drill's prose now says `SIGKILL`
and "types into panes", which costs nothing and removes the parser entirely.

### The three refusals that are safety properties, not usage niceties

1. **`--all` and `--drill` together are REFUSED, by name, before anything is armed or created.**
   `--drill` restricts to a list this drill wrote; `--all` would widen to every limited session a
   census finds. Handed both, a reader cannot say which won, and the accident is the widening one
   winning. rc 2, token `REFUSED:mutually-exclusive`.

2. **A sid without a seed stamp is refused BY NAME.** This is the shortest path from this file to a
   keystroke in a REAL working session: `--drill <manifest>` types into every session the manifest
   names. `--seed` writes a stamp per session it creates (`run=`, `sid=`, `throwaway=1`, `digest=`)
   and `check_manifest` accepts a row only when a stamp vouches for it AND the row's digest agrees
   with the stamp's — so a row edited to point a stamped entry at a different sid fails on the
   digest rather than silently retargeting.

   **Stated rather than implied: the stamp bounds ACCIDENT, not malice.** Anyone who can write
   `$LR_DRILL_STATE_DIR` can write a stamp. What it stops is a hand-edited manifest, a manifest
   copied from another run, and a sid pasted from a screenshot — which is the realistic threat and
   the one the brief names. A safety property whose threat model is unstated gets read as stronger
   than it is.

3. **The manifest's SHAPE is part of the contract** — five sessions, three in the shared checkout,
   two in worktrees, exactly one carrying an in-flight subagent, one arm each, no arm twice. A
   well-stamped four-session manifest would run a drill that silently does less than it claims,
   which the brief's hazard note calls worse than no drill at all.

### The five verdict mappers

Each fault arm has a PURE mapper over the stage output the drill recorded, so the parts an agent
may not run are still testable:

| arm | fault | canonical verdict | what the mapper reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) `gate` | `CC_ADMIT_HEADROOM_OVERRIDE` in the fire's environment | `FAILED:gate:<term>` | `relaunch.rc` = 9 · `gate.elapsed_ms` ≤ 3000 · the term |
| (b) `watcher` | the watcher SIGKILLed after `transplanted` | `STALE:watcher` | `watcher.armed_at`/`killed_at` · `reaper.state` · `keystrokes.log` |
| (c) `draft` | a draft typed into the composer, never submitted | `HELD:draft` | `precheck.verdict` · `moved.txt` |
| (d) `queued` | the fire lands behind a running turn | `queued->engaged` | `events.jsonl` · `engaged.verdict` |
| (e) `router` | all-thin accounts seeded under `CC_ACCOUNTS_BIN` | `PARKED:no-target` | `fleet.mech`/`fleet.note` · `rank.stderr` · `moved.txt` |

**All five arms prove ONE § 13 row, not five.** My first draft scattered them across rows 8–11, so
the watcher arm's verdict was being reported under "husks = 0" and the router arm's under "loaded:
ENGAGED ≤ 300 s". § 13's own table says it plainly — the row proved by "fault arms (a)–(e)" is the
single row *every failure named in a status view within 30 s and re-drivable*; rows 8–11 are proved
by a husk census, the recycle-intent ledger and an engaged-latency distribution, none of which is in
this file. Labelling an arm's verdict with another row's name is a drill claiming a measurement it
did not take, so row 3 now aggregates all five arms and distinguishes an arm that came back WRONG
(FAIL) from one that never recorded anything (UNMEASURED). Row 11 is the second measured row, from a
timed `fire()` helper that takes the WORST of five — "the call returns in ≤ 3 s" is a claim about
every fire, not about their average.

**Every mapper fails loud.** An absent or unreadable input yields `UNMEASURED:<why>` and rc 4,
never a healthy-looking verdict. `UNMEASURED` is carried as a THIRD state all the way through to
`--assert`, which exits 4 on it and 0 only when all twelve rows are present and PASS — because
collapsing "the instrument did not run" into "it passed" is the vacuous green this whole file
exists to make impossible. Absence is likewise not a pass: a missing row is rc 3.

### Arm (c) is the hardest assertion, and its hardness is `moved.txt`

The brief calls it the arm that could not pass in any of the three designs. Its content is not "the
precheck said HELD" — it is "**nothing moved**": no split-brain lock, no tombstone, no
`.jsonl.handed-off` rename. So `probe_movement` writes `moved.txt` **unconditionally, even when
empty**, and the mapper treats its ABSENCE as `UNMEASURED`, not as emptiness. An emptiness gate
cannot separate "nothing moved" from "nobody looked", and the two demand opposite actions
(`docs/lessons/empty-vs-no-surface.md`). That distinction has its own case and its own mutant.

## 3. What the spec got wrong

**(a) `FAILED:gate:headroom` — the refusing TERM is not in the text the launcher writes.** This is
the sharpest of the three and it would have made the arm permanently `UNMEASURED`.
`scripts/lib/capacity-admit.sh` builds `CC_ADMIT_REASON` as
`capacity-admit: REFUSING <what> — <detail> (refusal n of N; …)` and passes the term NAME as a
separate argument that reaches only the IDL row (`term:`). `lr-fire-resume` quotes that reason into
`relaunch.rc`'s state record, so the record names the numbers — `reclaimable 0.01GB < floor 4096GB`
— and never the word `headroom`. A mapper that grepped the reason for the term would answer
`UNMEASURED` on a perfectly good refusal. **Resolution:** `m_term` reads the IDL's own `term` field
first (collected by `collect_run_state`) and falls back to the detail SHAPE, keyed on the five
`detail=` literals capacity-admit builds, one per term. Both paths have a case.

**(b) There is no `engaged` state.** § 12 W7 writes arm (d)'s expectation as "`queued` then
`engaged`", which reads as two states of one log. The run log's submit stage writes `submitted` OR
`queued` OR `FAILED:submit` — three sibling branches of ONE decision in `lr-fire-resume.sh:969-980`
— and engagement is a different question answered by a different instrument
(`hooks/lib/engagement.sh`), whose result reaches `~/.claude/logs/handoffs.jsonl` as a
`recycle-engaged` row. **Resolution:** the mapper reads two inputs, and the second is the shipped
predicate rather than a state string the spec wished for.

**(c) `STALE` and `PARKED:no-target` are composed, not quoted.** Of the five verdicts § 12 W7 names,
only `HELD:draft` is a literal in the tree. `STALE` appears in the poller as `RUN-CLAIM-STALE` /
`STALE:boot`; there is no `PARKED:no-target` anywhere — `lr-fleet` writes mechanism cell `parked`
with the note `no routable target`. The mappers therefore COMPOSE the canonical strings from what
the tree records, and say so in the code. A mapper that invented a string the tree never writes
would be asserting its own vocabulary back to itself.

**(d) Arm (b)'s reaper does not exist on this tree, and the drill says so instead of pretending.**
§ 12 W7 expects "reaper `STALE` next tick". `scripts/limit-recover/lr-reset-poller.sh:709` states in
its own words that *"the run REAPER is the sibling plan's and is out of scope"*, and the only stale
marker that exists is a **30-minute TTL** on the run claim (`RUN_CLAIM_TTL_MIN`, `:714`), logged as
`RUN-CLAIM-STALE`. There is no next-tick reaper to observe. `collect_reaper_state` reads the
poller's own log if that line ever appears, and the arm records `UNMEASURED` when it does not.

**The other half of arm (b) IS satisfiable and is the half that matters** — no keystroke while a
watcher pid is still alive; two actuators on one pane is the failure D3's critique calls fatal. Two
corrections made it real rather than vacuous: the window now OPENS BEFORE the fire (a window opened
at the instant of the kill is one `date` call wide and the assertion inside it is free), and the
keystrokes compared against it are the **surface's own typing records** parsed out of the run log
(`stage` ∈ {inject, submit, relaunch}, `state` ∈ {relaunch-typed, SUBMIT-RECR, READY-QUIET}), not
what the drill itself typed. See residual R5 for what that still does not cover.

**(e) `lr_is_husk` does not exist.** § 13's "zero silent husks" row cites `lr_is_husk = 0` as its
proof. That function is a D3 DESIGN artifact (`design/D3-identity-observability.md:280`) and appears
in no shipped file — verified by a tree-wide grep, which returns only `docs/`. Nothing in this wave
can call it. The husk row is one of the seven this drill marks OPERATOR-VERIFIED (§ 5).

**(f) The drill row numbers are cited but never enumerated.** §§ 12–13 cite "drill row 4", "rows
5–7, 10", "rows 5–10", "rows 8–9", "row 11", "row 12" — six citations, no table anywhere in the
plan. I reconstructed a twelve-row table that satisfies every citation and made it the SSOT for
both `--drill` (which writes a row per line) and `--assert` (which refuses a results.tsv missing
any of them). `tests/lr-drill.sh --rows` prints it. The reconstruction is a JUDGEMENT, not a
finding, and a later editor who disagrees should change `drill_rows()` and nothing else.

**(g) § 13's numbers predate the wave they now measure.** Flagged as the brief asked: the
expected-numbers table was measured 2026-09-19, BEFORE W5 landed. The drill quotes it as the target,
not as a re-measurement, and a failing row is as likely to indict the number as the tree. That
sentence is in the code, above `drill_rows()`, not only here.

### What the five survivors were actually worth

**M24 is the one that mattered, and it found a defect rather than a missing case.** `map_router`'s
"a park must carry the router's own reasons" clause was **unreachable**: `m_read` returns rc 1 for
an absent file *and* for an empty one (`[ -s ]`), so an empty `rank.stderr` became `UNMEASURED` one
line above and the `FAIL` could never fire. Worse, the case that "covered" it asserted **rc 4** —
it had encoded the dead behaviour as its expectation, which is `docs/lessons`'
stale-assertion-becomes-an-inverted-guard in miniature, in code I had written that same hour. The
two states are opposite answers: **no file** = the drill never captured the router's stderr
(UNMEASURED, retry); **an empty file** = the router was asked and named nothing (FAIL). Split, one
case per arm, plus M24b to pin that they stay split. Mutation found this; reading did not.

**M13, M22, M26 were fixture blind spots** — each a combination my fixtures happened never to
produce, and each with a one-line argument for why it is real: `--stamp` is a format writer and
will stamp *any* string, so the stamp check never shadowed the uuid check; `submitted` and `queued`
are sibling branches of one decision, so a fire that went straight through is a healthy recovery
and a failed drill arm; a mechanism cell that is not `parked` means the all-thin fixture did not
take.

**M40 is the instructive one about fixture design.** Row 11's case put the maximum in the LAST
slot, where "take the maximum" and "take the last value you saw" agree exactly — so the comparison
could be replaced by a bare assignment and nothing died. The maximum is now asserted from both
ends. The general form: *an ordered reduction must be exercised with its extremum somewhere other
than the position the naive implementation would also land on.*

**No survivor was argued away as an equivalence guard.** All five are closed by cases.

Four of those went red first and are worth recording rather than smoothing over:

  `A && B` setup lines. The house idiom is `[[ … ]] || false` (46 uses in `tests/cc-lr-front.bats`),

  the function — so three row-3 cases saw an arm with no verdict file and went red for a reason

  mf="…manifest-$run.tsv"` leaves `mf` as `manifest-.tsv`: every argument of the `local` builtin is
  word-expanded BEFORE the builtin runs, so `"$run"` reads the OUTER `run` (unset). Measured on both
  `/bin/bash 3.2` and `bash 5.3`. Both fixture calls therefore wrote ONE file and the second
  silently replaced the first, which is exactly the kind of fixture defect that makes a suite agree
  with itself. Split into two statements, with the measurement in a comment beside it.
