# g2 — Opus 5.5 vs Opus 5 token spend at the same effort (per task, per turn)

Gap question: at the same effort level, how many more/fewer tokens does Opus 5.5 spend than Opus 5, per task and per turn?

## Method
- Text: pdftotext pp.148-151, 174-180, 209-212; prompting.txt, whatsnew.txt, effort.txt, migration.txt, announce.txt (grep + full sections).
- Charts: extracted the NATIVE embedded images (pdfimages; 2000x1300 px) for Fig 8.4.A (p.176), 8.4.B (p.177), 8.8.A (p.180), 8.14.6.A (p.212), plus /tmp/opus55-src/figs/fig-150-070.png (p.150). Located marker centres by colour (Opus 5.5 = RGB 232,104,48; Opus 5 = 232,160,0; Fable 5.1 = 24,168,120; Fable 5 = 40,120,208) and converted pixels to values by the axis tick marks (log axes: FrontierCode 5k/10k/20k/30k/50k/100k/150k ticks at x=123.5/478.5/833.5/1040.5/1301.5/1656.5/1863.5 -> 1178 px/decade; CursorBench $1/$2/$5/$10/$20 ticks -> 1294 px/decade; CoT chart 1k/2k/.../10k/20k ticks -> 850 px/decade; AutomationBench linear $0.40@233 .. $1.80@1897).
- CALIBRATION CHECK: every measured y value that the text prints matched to 0.1 pt (FrontierCode Main Opus 5.5 medium 54.6, max 54.4; Opus 5 medium 53.4; Fable 5.1 low 52.8; Extended Opus 5.5 medium 65.3, max 63.6; Opus 5 medium 63.6). The CursorBench x-calibration reproduced the printed costs (Opus 5 max $11.95 exactly; Fable 5.1 max $17.3 vs printed $17.28). So the pixel readings are accurate to roughly +/-3% on x.
- CORRECTION to the base (c1 notes F9/F10): pixel reading puts Opus 5.5 MAX on FrontierCode Main at ~166k output tokens/task (c1 said ~155k; ~155k is closer to the EXTENDED value, ~144k), Opus 5 xhigh on Main at ~67k (c1: ~62k), Opus 5 medium Main ~34k (c1: ~33k). Everything else agrees within ~5%.
- CORRECTION to the brief: the p.150 CoT chart jump between Opus 5.5's 3rd and 4th settings is ~15-17x, not ~30x; ~30x is lowest-to-highest (1st->4th).

Derived (INFERENCE, arithmetic on the readings):
- Same ratio profile on both task sets: ~0.41-0.42x at low, 0.55x at medium, 0.47x at high, ~0.9x at xhigh, 1.68x at max.
- Within-model escalation: Opus 5.5 low->max = ~18-19x output tokens; Opus 5 low->max = ~4.5-4.8x. Opus 5.5 xhigh->max = ~2.8-2.9x (Opus 5: ~1.5x). Opus 5.5 high->xhigh = ~2.3x (Opus 5: ~1.2x).
- Cross-level matching: Opus 5.5 at medium (18.5k) spends ~1/3 of Opus 5 high (55.4k) and ~0.55x Opus 5 medium, at a higher score than both. Opus 5.5 at high (26.2k) spends less than Opus 5 at medium (33.6k).
- Opus 5.5's own spread is wider: its low is the cheapest Claude point on the chart, its max is the most expensive point on the chart (both Main and Extended) — beyond every other model's max.
- Scores decline above medium on this benchmark (text p.176: grading penalises out-of-scope changes), so the extra tokens at xhigh/max buy nothing here.

Text p.176 (verbatim): "Opus 5.5's highest FrontierCode scores—54.6% on Main and 65.3% on Extended—are at medium effort. Scores decline above medium effort but mostly recover at max". Note p.176: "There is a decline in model performance on FrontierCode (Main and Extended) above medium effort. The evaluation is designed around writing mergeable code diffs without requiring human edits, so its grading penalizes out-of-scope changes".
Caption: "Cognition's result files report token counts and no cost, so the x-axis shows average output tokens per task on a logarithmic scale."

## B. Per task, as COST (tokens x list price) — Opus 5.5 vs Opus 5 at each effort

|---|---|---|---|---|

| medium | $2.90 / 52.5 | $6.94 / ~43.4 | 0.42x | $7.06 / ~46.8 |

| xhigh | $6.99 / 56.0 | $11.43 / ~46.1 | 0.61x | $13.01 / ~51.6 |

## C. Per turn — what the docs say (no figure anywhere)
- prompting.txt L48: "At a given level, Claude Opus 5.5 tends to think more per turn than Claude Opus 5, especially at xhigh and max. If you keep the effort value you set for Claude Opus 5, expect longer turns and more output tokens."
- whatsnew.txt L90: "At the same effort setting the model tends to think more per turn than Claude Opus 5, most of all at xhigh and max. Re-run your effort sweep rather than carrying a setting over, and leave room in max_tokens for the thinking."
- prompting.txt L46: "Effort level names don't correspond to the same amount of thinking across models: in Anthropic's testing, Claude Opus 5.5 at medium matches or exceeds Claude Opus 5 at high on coding and knowledge-work evaluations, and on several coding evaluations low comes close to it at much lower cost."
- prompting.txt L50: max_tokens must leave room for thinking; "a limit sized for Claude Opus 5 with thinking off can cut replies off. For the long turns that agentic coding can produce, a max_tokens of 128,000, the model's maximum, has worked well".
- effort.txt L131: for Opus 5 at xhigh/max "Starting at 64k tokens and tuning from there is a reasonable default." (vs 128k recommended for Opus 5.5 long agentic turns).
- migration.txt L31: Opus 5.5 "keeps Claude Opus 5's 1M token context window and 128k max output tokens".
- prompting.txt L39: at default medium the model matched or beat Opus 5 at high "in fewer steps and with fewer tokens" on multistep repo work.
- prompting.txt L17: "tends to finish the same task with fewer tokens" and "generates output tokens more than 30 percent faster".
- prompting.txt L60: "At low the model keeps its thinking short. How often it skips thinking altogether depends on your prompts".
- effort.txt L119 / whatsnew L88: default is medium on Opus 5.5 vs high on Opus 5 — "a request that omits effort runs one level lower than it did on Claude Opus 5".
- effort.txt L200: "In a tool-use loop, follow-up requests that only process tool results can still skip thinking at any level."
- Reconciliation (INFERENCE): "more thinking per turn" + "fewer tokens per task" + "fewer steps" are consistent only if Opus 5.5 takes substantially fewer turns per task; no source quantifies turns/task or tokens/turn for either model. The FrontierCode per-task crossover near xhigh/max matches the docs' "especially at xhigh and max".
