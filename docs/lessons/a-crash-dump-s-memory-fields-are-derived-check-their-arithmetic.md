# A crash dump's memory fields are DERIVED; check their arithmetic before quoting one

_Relocated VERBATIM from `.claude/rules/agent-operating-lessons.md` (always-loaded tier) — the rules file now carries the hook and links here. Nothing was shortened._

[A crash dump's memory fields are DERIVED; check their arithmetic before quoting one](.) — 2026-09-16, the double watchdog panic (`docs/research/kernel-watchdog-panic-2026-09-16.md`): a panic log's per-process `residentMemoryBytes` summed to **269.8 GB on a 64 GB box** — 4.2× physical, which is impossible for resident pages and is the tell. It is the task's **phys_footprint, and it counts COMPRESSED pages at their UNCOMPRESSED size**: the arithmetic closes exactly, 252.2 GB stored-in-compressor (16,527,733 pages × 16 KB, from the sentinel's own `vm_stat`) + 16.8 GB active/inactive = the 269.8 GB total. The asymmetry across processes is the confirmation, not a coincidence: `WindowServer` reads 1,528 MB in the panic against 1,571–1,681 MB in a live `ps` minutes earlier (little of it compressible), while each `clang-format` reads **27 GB in the panic against ~700 MB in `ps` at the same storm** (nearly all of it compressed away). ⇒ **Two consequences that point in opposite directions, and both bit.** (a) For PRICING a compressor exhaustion the footprint figure is the RIGHT unit — it is precisely the anonymous demand that fills the segment table — but it must be NAMED as footprint; calling it "resident memory" is a claim that is false on its face against the machine's RAM. (b) For a `ps`-based ACTUATOR the same processes are nearly invisible: an RSS floor is measuring the one quantity the compressor is actively hiding, so a storm whose resident share falls below the floor while its footprint climbs evades selection entirely. Second field, same class and cheaper to check: **`memoryStatus.memoryPressureDetails.pagesWanted` is not a demand measurement**, it is `vm_page_free_target − free_count` (4000 − 906 = **3094, IDENTICAL in two independent panics 35 minutes apart**), so a "reclaim efficiency" computed as reclaimed/wanted describes the sysctl constant and not the incident — the figure `1.7% reclaim` was quoted, believed, and had to be withdrawn. ⇒ **In any crash dump, before a memory number becomes an argument: sum it against physical RAM, and look for the field repeating bit-for-bit across unrelated incidents.** A quantity that cannot vary is a constant wearing a measurement's clothes. Companion, and the reason this investigation had controls at all: **when the fault RECURS while you are investigating it, the replication is a free natural experiment and its discriminators are whatever CHANGED** — here one repeat 35 minutes later refuted three live hypotheses at once with no new instrument (wired 20.22 → **3.57 GB**, `last started kext` smbfs → **autofs**, process count 1,032 → **815** against 1,019 at healthy idle), where each would otherwise have needed its own argument.
