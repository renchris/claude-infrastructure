# A ranker preferring LESS headroom may be pricing DECAY, not headroom

_Relocated VERBATIM from `.claude/rules/agent-operating-lessons.md` (always-loaded tier) — the rules file now carries the hook and links here. Nothing was shortened._

[A ranker preferring LESS headroom may be pricing DECAY, not headroom](.) — 2026-09-12, recovering two weekly-capped panes: `claude-accounts --rank general` put **next3 (weekly 82% used, 18% left)** above **next2 (4% used, 96% left)**, which reads as an obvious defect — recovering *off* a weekly cap *into* the account nearest its own cap invites a second wall — and I overrode it, targeting next2 by hand. The override was WRONG and the ranker was right. Its score is `headroom / T**γ`, γ=2 (`bin/claude-accounts:1715`), and the comment one line down names this exact pair: *"Linear headroom/T was indifferent between {19% left, resets 27.8h} and {87% left, resets 122h} — the required burn RATES match — but stranding is not rate-symmetric: quota expiring tomorrow has no later chance, quota with 5 days keeps every one of them."* It is **use-it-or-lose-it**, adversarially verified, and the algebraic form of the operator's own standing rule that unused weekly quota is decaying inventory that does not roll over. So the visible term (headroom) is the NUMERATOR of a two-term score, and reading it alone inverts the verdict: the account with less left is preferred precisely BECAUSE its remainder expires sooner. My proposed "improvement" — have lr-fleet's `auto` prefer maximum headroom for the recovery lane — would have restored the linear behaviour that formula deliberately replaced, and shipped as a fix. ⇒ **before overriding a scorer on an intuition about one visible input, read the score's OTHER term.** A ranking that looks wrong on the axis you can see is the normal appearance of a scorer pricing an axis you have not looked for — and a scorer with a `_EXP`/γ/weight constant and a kill switch (`CC_ROUTE_URGENCY_EXP=1`) is announcing that its shape was argued over, so the burden is on the overrider. Cheap discriminator: `--rank` prints the SCORES, not just the order (next3 0.000042 vs next2 0.000039 — a 7% gap, not a landslide), so a near-tie is the tell that the terms are trading against each other rather than one dominating. Companion, separable: **a per-lane override of a global router needs a reason the GLOBAL router cannot already express** — "this lane is different" is a hypothesis, and here the lane was not different at all; the recovery is cheap and repeatable (two panes, ~90s, zero re-spend), which is exactly the cost model under which burning the decaying account first is correct.
