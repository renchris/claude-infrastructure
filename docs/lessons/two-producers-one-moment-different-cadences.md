[Two producers, one moment, different cadences](.) — 2026-09-09, cc-backlog `02c8b96e55b9` closed REFUTED by disproof `8095e40d3d2e` (`docs/research/beat-vs-telemetry-cadence-2026-09-09.md`): **observing producer A fresh and producer B stale AT THE SAME MOMENT localises a fault only if the two share a cadence — and "they both write per turn" is exactly the kind of thing that is written in a COMMENT and executed by nothing.** The item reasoned that `/tmp/cc-telemetry/<sid>.json` fresh beside `~/.claude/cc-beats/<sid>.json` hours stale proved the beat producer specifically was dead, since "both are per-turn-boundary writes for the SAME session at the SAME moment". They are not: the beat is `hooks/session-beat.sh` at UserPromptSubmit/Stop (true boundaries), while the telemetry is the **statusline**, re-run on every render — measured live inside one turn, `telem_mtime` advanced between two consecutive tool calls while the beat held at `seq=1` for 183 s. So the divergence is the ORDINARY signature of a session inside a long turn. The filing session's own numbers close the arithmetic exactly: its largest prompt-boundary gap was 4.66 h (15:44:02Z → 20:23:27Z), it was filed at 20:10:48Z *inside* that gap, and the refusal it quoted reported the beat as 15,937 s = 4.43 h old — `20:10:48 − 15:44:02` to the minute. Nothing was frozen, and the store proves it: the sid called "frozen at seq=8" now reads **seq=59**. ⇒ **before inferring a fault from two signals disagreeing, measure each one's cadence — and note the inference fails in BOTH directions.** The item also claimed its evidence ELIMINATED "a session-wide dead/erroring hook"; the telemetry writer is the statusline and **not a hook at all**, so its liveness could never have acquitted the hook layer. A confounded observation neither convicts nor acquits. **Second half, the expensive one and separately reusable: a gate's refusal message NAMES A CAUSE, and that name becomes the next reader's hypothesis.** `bin/cc-await-ping`'s `stale-beat` refusal asserted "an arm happens INSIDE a turn, so a live beat producer makes this seconds old — a stale one means either the producer is not running or the sid is not this session's". An arm *does* happen inside a turn, which is precisely why it fails: `now − beat.t` at arm time is the age of THIS TURN'S OPENING BEAT, i.e. how long the turn has been running — unbounded on a healthy session — so a dead producer and a long turn drive it up identically and the statistic cannot separate them (the corpus already names this: *freshness is relative to the SUBJECT, not the clock*). Measured over every real emission on the box, **9 of 9** refused sessions had a producer that beat to the session's very last record (`beat.t` within 64–520 s of the transcript end): **zero true positives**, and one message cost a 20-day dispatch cycle hunting a producer that was never broken. ⇒ **the fix for a confounded gate is usually NOT to loosen it.** The refusal stays (a false pass arms the starvation pole; a false refusal merely declines to park, the wanted polarity under a goal) — what changes is that it stops naming a refuted cause and instead reports the fields that DO separate the states (`seq`, `kind`): a climbing `seq` across reads is a live producer in a long turn, a frozen one is the real fault. Third, cheap and nearly shipped: the first draft of this note priced the gate at "~1% of arms: 61 against 5,632" by folding `verdict=delivered` into the denominator — but the BARE form of the tool emits that too, so it is a different population; the honest idle-scoped denominator is `stood-down` (446) against `stale-beat` (71). **When you quote a rate for one mode of a multi-mode tool, check that every term in the denominator could only have come from that mode.**
