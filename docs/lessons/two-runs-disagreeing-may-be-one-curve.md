# Two runs disagreeing may be one curve

_Relocated VERBATIM from `.claude/rules/agent-operating-lessons.md` (always-loaded tier) — the rules file now carries the hook and links here. Nothing was shortened._

[Two runs disagreeing may be one curve](.) — 2026-09-09, cc-backlog `34b35fc074d7`, landed 5ab97f7d6 + 18a6d99fa: two sessions ran the SAME published one-line benchmark on the SAME box two hours apart and got **2.90x** and **5.98x**. That reads as a rig that cannot repeat, and the natural move is to adjudicate — pick the better-run one, or call the rig unreliable. Both are wrong. Restricted to the ambient band the two runs SHARED (8.50..13.00 runnable) they agree: **3.41x against 3.53x**. They were not conflicting measurements of one quantity; they were two samples of a **monotone curve at non-overlapping x**, and the covariate was ambient load — which nobody had modelled as a covariate at all, because it was in the header as a *precondition* ("run it on a quiet box"), not as an axis. ⇒ **before adjudicating between two runs that disagree, plot each against every nuisance variable you recorded and check whether their ranges even overlap.** A precondition you can only partly satisfy is a covariate in disguise. Second half, mechanically separate and the reason it bit: **a warning about a nuisance variable must be a property of the ESTIMATE, not of the SAMPLE.** The bench already carried `⚠ AMBIENT MOVED >2x`, which is `max/min` over the idle arm — the *exact* shape f229870cc had removed from the acceptance gate one screen above it for being non-decreasing in n — and it says the box MOVED while saying nothing about whether the moving reached the number. So both runs tripped it and both were reasoned past it on a passing null control, and a figure ~2x too high landed on trunk. The replacement is `Spearman(ambient, ratio)` at one-sided p<0.05, whose false-positive rate under a true null is flat in n while its power rises — the correct polarity, and the discriminator a range can never be: a fixture whose ambient swings 2.1x with a FLAT ratio trips the old check and leaves the new one silent. ⇒ **when you fix a gate for being a sample statistic, grep the same file for the others** — the sibling checks are written by the same hand on the same day and rot the same way. Third: two mechanisms predicted that same positive correlation — a real `O(load)` queueing gain (the bench own thesis) and a denominator artifact — so the direction was set by a **discriminating arm**, not by whichever story was in hand: the estimator divides by the SMALL arm, and pooled over 33 cycles Spearman(ambient, serial attributable) = **-0.566** against parallel = **+0.273**, with **completed dispatches FLAT across ambient terciles** (243/243/249 vs 735/745/724). Equal work at every ambient level is what makes "the denominator collapsed" the only reading left; without that throughput control the two accounts are observationally identical. Fourth, cheap and separately costly: **a published command with a hardcoded `/tmp` output path is a silent data-loss bug the moment two workers exist** — the sibling run overwrote both raw TSVs, and `--analyse <path>` then returns the OTHER run numbers under the earlier run name, no error and no tell.
